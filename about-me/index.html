Independent researcher

Why my name is Chen Xianhe (陈先河)

I come from Wangcheng Town, Xinjian County, Nanchang, Jiangxi Province, China. Before moving abroad, I grew up in Bao'an District, Shenzhen (ages 0–9), and Xinjian, Nanchang (ages 10–19).

My name has three parts: Chen (陈) is my family name, Xian (先) is my generation character, and He (河) is my personal name. When my parents named me, they drew on Taoist ideas about the five elements. I was believed to lack the Water element, so they chose 河 (He), whose three-dot water radical (氵) was meant to make up for it.

Many people read Xianhe (先河) as a single given name. Xian He (先河) also happens to be a Chinese word for a pioneering first: something that sets a precedent, breaks new ground, or is the first of its kind.

How to pronounce my name?

The pinyin is Chén Xiān Hé. The "ch" in Chen sounds like the "ch" in Jackie Chan. My family name is pronounced "Chen" in Mandarin and "Chan" in Cantonese. Xian begins like the xi in xiexie (谢谢). In this simple English approximation, the "e" in Chen, the "an" in Xian, and the "e" in He all sound like "uh" (the schwa sound /ə/). He therefore sounds like "huh".

Using the Cambridge Dictionary's IPA symbols, my name can be approximated as /tʃən ʃjən hə/.
